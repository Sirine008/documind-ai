Home Coffee Brewing Guide

This short guide explains how to brew better coffee at home with simple equipment.

Grind size. The grind size should match the brewing method. Espresso needs a fine grind, pour-over needs a medium-fine grind, and the French press needs a coarse grind. A grind that is too fine for the method causes over-extraction and a bitter taste, while a grind that is too coarse causes under-extraction and a sour taste.

Water. Use water that is just off the boil, between 90 and 96 degrees Celsius. Filtered water often improves taste because chlorine and heavy minerals can mask the flavor of the coffee.

Ratio. A good starting ratio is 1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams of water. For a single large mug of about 300 grams of water, use roughly 19 grams of coffee. Adjust the ratio to taste after a few attempts.

French press method. Add coarse coffee to the press, pour hot water over it, stir gently and wait four minutes. Then press the plunger down slowly and serve immediately, because coffee left in the press keeps extracting and becomes bitter.

Storage. Store whole beans in an airtight container away from light, heat and moisture. Coffee tastes best within about one month of its roast date. Grinding beans just before brewing preserves aromas that are lost quickly after grinding.

Cleaning. Rinse equipment after every use and clean it thoroughly once a week. Coffee oils build up over time and give later brews a stale, rancid taste.
